SAP to Infor migration: field service data and workflow

What moving from SAP to Infor actually involves for field service teams — data mapping, timeline, effort range, and the hardest part.

Why teams move from SAP

SAP's high TCO, long implementation cycles, and requirement for dedicated BASIS staff lead teams to move when they cannot justify the support overhead or when their operation is below the scale SAP is designed for.

What you're moving to: Infor

Infor CloudSuite is a ERP platform from Infor (Koch Industries), best suited for Mid-market to Enterprise (250+ employees) organisations in Healthcare, Manufacturing, Distribution, Fashion. Strengths relevant to this migration: Industry-specific micro-verticals (Healthcare, Fashion, Food); Birst embedded analytics. Known limitations to factor in: Smaller ecosystem; M3 and LN legacy products still common; support quality uneven.

The hardest part of this migration

Data extraction from SAP: SAP stores field service data in highly normalised PM/CS module tables with custom Z-table extensions. Extracting clean work order history with all linked parts, labour confirmations, and measurement documents requires ABAP extraction scripts — not a simple CSV export.

This is the step that consistently takes longer than planned. Budget time for a data audit before any tooling decisions — the quality and structure of your SAP data determines whether the migration runs in months or quarters.

Migration sequence

  1. Data audit (weeks 1–3). Extract a sample of SAP records. Assess completeness, duplicates, and schema gaps. This is where the effort estimate gets refined — everything that follows depends on data quality.
  2. Target system configuration (weeks 2–8, parallel). Configure Infor before any data arrives: work order types, skill definitions, SLA rules, dispatch board layout, and mobile app settings. Data migration into an unconfigured system creates rework.
  3. Historical data migration (weeks 6–14). Migrate closed work orders, customer records, asset register, and technician profiles. Validate record counts and spot-check a sample of complex records.
  4. Integration cutover (weeks 10–16). Connect Infor to your billing system, ERP, and any other adjacent tools. Test the full job lifecycle — intake to invoice — end to end before go-live.
  5. Parallel run (4–8 weeks). Dispatch from Infor. Maintain SAP as a read-only reference. Reconcile daily. This is the phase that teams consistently underestimate — budget the dispatcher time to run both systems simultaneously.
  6. Cutover. Freeze SAP data. Complete delta migration of records created during parallel run. Go live. Retain SAP read-only access for 90 days minimum.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a SAP to Infor migration take?

9–24 months from kickoff to cutover, including parallel run. The variable is data complexity and integration count. Clean, well-structured SAP data migrates faster than heavily customised instances with years of accumulated work order history.

What is the hardest part of migrating from SAP to Infor?

SAP stores field service data in highly normalised PM/CS module tables with custom Z-table extensions. Extracting clean work order history with all linked parts, labour confirmations, and measurement documents requires ABAP extraction scripts — not a simple CSV export. Beyond the technical extraction, the hardest operational challenge is the parallel run — running two dispatch systems simultaneously while keeping records reconciled.

Can we migrate open work orders from SAP to Infor?

In-flight work orders at cutover are the most difficult records to migrate cleanly. Most teams handle these manually: freeze SAP at cutover, complete a snapshot of open orders, and create them fresh in Infor. Automated migration of open work orders with live technician assignments rarely works without significant reconciliation effort.
