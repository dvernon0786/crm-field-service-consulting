Service dispatch software

What dispatch software costs, what it does, and when a standalone tool beats buying the full FSM suite.

Service dispatch software costs $20–$200 per dispatcher per month for dedicated tools. Most small-to-mid dispatch teams pay $50–$120/month per dispatcher seat.

What service dispatch software does

Dispatch software solves one problem: getting the right technician to the right job at the right time. The core capability is a live dispatch board that shows who is available, where they are, and what they're skilled at — then makes the assignment, either manually (drag-and-drop) or automatically (AI-driven scheduling).

Every capable dispatch tool includes:

  • Live dispatch board with real-time technician status and location
  • Drag-and-drop job assignment (manual dispatch)
  • Automated scheduling based on skills, location, and availability
  • GPS tracking and route optimisation
  • Two-way messaging between dispatch and field technicians
  • Customer notification when the technician is en route
  • Job history log by technician and customer

What separates a standalone dispatch tool from a full field service management platform is everything around this core: work order management, parts inventory, invoicing, customer portal, and SLA tracking. If you already have systems covering those areas, a standalone dispatch tool may be all you need and significantly cheaper.

What drives the price up

Automated scheduling vs manual dispatch

Manual dispatch (a human picks the technician from a board) is included in every tool. AI-driven or constraint-based automated scheduling — which considers skills, certifications, part availability, SLA urgency, and geography simultaneously — is a premium feature. For teams running 20+ technicians and 100+ jobs per day, automated scheduling typically recovers its cost in reduced drive time. For smaller teams, manual dispatch is sufficient.

Real-time GPS and route optimisation

Included at most platforms. The add-on is live traffic integration and dynamic re-routing when a job runs long — some vendors charge separately for real-time traffic data feeds.

Volume of jobs per day

Some platforms price on transaction volume rather than seat count. If your team runs 500+ work orders per day, check whether the platform prices per user or per transaction — the model matters significantly at that volume.

What to check before you buy

  1. How does it receive new jobs? Web form, API, phone call logged manually, email parsing, or integration with your CRM — this is where teams hit friction. Confirm the intake method works for how your customers actually contact you.
  2. Dispatcher seat vs technician seat pricing. Some platforms charge dispatchers significantly more than field technicians. A team with 5 dispatchers and 40 technicians has a very different bill than a team with 1 dispatcher and 44 technicians.
  3. Offline mobile access. If your technicians work in basements, underground facilities, or remote sites, offline mode is not optional. Confirm it works for your specific use case — offline implementations vary widely in capability across vendors.
  4. Integration with your work order or billing system. A dispatch tool that cannot push a completed job record to your invoicing system creates manual re-entry work. Confirm the integration exists and is maintained, not just theoretically possible via a generic API.

Frequently asked questions

What does service dispatch software cost?

$20–$200 per dispatcher per month for standalone dispatch tools. Dispatch as a module within a broader FSM platform runs $50–$300 per user per month. Most small-to-mid dispatch operations (under 50 technicians) pay $50–$120/month per dispatcher seat for a capable tool.

What is service dispatch software?

Software that assigns incoming service requests to field technicians based on availability, location, skills, and SLA requirements. Core features: live dispatch board, GPS tracking, automated or manual job assignment, route optimisation, and two-way messaging. May be a standalone tool or a module within a full field service management platform.

What is the difference between dispatch software and field service management software?

Dispatch software focuses on the assignment moment — getting the right tech to the right job. FSM software covers the full job lifecycle: work order creation, dispatch, completion tracking, parts, invoicing, and customer history. Most FSM platforms include dispatch; standalone dispatch tools integrate with separate work order and billing systems.

How many dispatchers do I need?

A rough benchmark: one dispatcher can manage 8–15 field technicians manually, or 20–40 technicians with automated scheduling turned on. These ratios vary significantly by industry — utilities and telecoms (planned, repeatable jobs) run leaner than HVAC or facilities maintenance (reactive, variable job types).
