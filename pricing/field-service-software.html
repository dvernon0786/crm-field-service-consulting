Field service software

Pricing, what separates the tiers, and how to choose without overpaying for features you won't use.

Field service software costs $20–$300 per user per month depending on platform and modules. Most small-to-mid-sized teams land between $50 and $150/user/month all-in.

Field service software vs field service management software

Vendors use these terms interchangeably. In practice, "field service software" often refers to the scheduling and mobile layer — what technicians use on a phone or tablet to see job details, capture photos, collect signatures, and log time. "Field service management software" usually implies the full operational picture including work orders, inventory, customer history, SLA tracking, and financial reporting.

Modern platforms cover both. The distinction matters when evaluating: some vendors offer a standalone scheduling and dispatch tool (lower cost, faster to implement) that integrates with your existing ERP or CRM. Others sell a full suite where FSM is one module among many. The right choice depends on what you already have running.

The three decisions that determine your price

1. Standalone vs suite

A standalone FSM tool costs less and implements faster, but creates an integration burden: job data needs to flow to your accounting system, customer records need to sync with your CRM, and parts inventory needs to connect to procurement. Each integration is a project in itself. A suite where FSM, ERP, and CRM are one system eliminates that — but you pay for features you may not use and accept a longer implementation.

2. Per-user vs consumption pricing

Most vendors charge per named user per month. Acumatica charges on consumption (transactions and compute), which can be significantly cheaper for teams with a large field technician headcount relative to office users. At 50+ field technicians, run the numbers on both models — the gap can be material.

3. Mobile capability requirements

Basic mobile apps (job details, status updates, signature capture) come standard. Advanced mobile features — offline mode for no-signal environments, IoT device integration, AR-assisted diagnostics, GPS tracking with geofencing — add cost and implementation time. Buy only what your technicians will actually use; the most sophisticated mobile feature is the one your team ignores.

What to ask for before you sign

  1. Total cost of ownership over 3 years, not just the monthly licence. Year 1 is always the most expensive; get a 3-year model so you can compare platforms on equal terms.
  2. Implementation scope and assumptions — specifically, what is and is not included in the implementation quote. Data migration, integrations, training, and change management are frequently scoped separately.
  3. Support tier and SLA — 24/7 support vs business hours, and what the escalation path is for a P1 incident when your dispatchers cannot see any jobs.
  4. Contract flexibility — annual vs multi-year, and whether you can add users without renegotiating. SMB teams grow and need headroom without a price renegotiation every 6 months.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between field service software and field service management software?

Vendors use the terms interchangeably. In practice, "field service software" typically refers to the scheduling and mobile layer; "field service management software" implies the full operational loop including work orders, inventory, and reporting. Modern platforms cover both — the distinction matters more for budget and implementation scope than for categorisation.

What does field service software cost per month?

$20–$300 per user per month depending on the platform. Entry-level (Odoo, Acumatica): $20–$80. Mid-market (Dynamics 365, Salesforce, NetSuite): $75–$180. Enterprise (SAP, IFS, ServiceNow): $100–$300+. Implementation adds 1–3× the first-year licence on top.

Can field service software integrate with my existing CRM or ERP?

Yes, though the effort varies. Same-vendor integrations (Salesforce FSM + Salesforce CRM; Dynamics 365 FSM + Dynamics 365 Sales) are typically configuration work, not custom development. Cross-vendor integrations require middleware — Azure Integration Services, MuleSoft, or custom API work — and add 2–6 months to the timeline.

How long does it take to implement field service software?

2–9 months for SMB platforms (Odoo, Acumatica); 4–18 months for mid-market (Dynamics 365, Salesforce, NetSuite); 9–36 months for enterprise (SAP, IFS). The variable that matters most is not the platform but the number of integrations and the state of your existing data.
